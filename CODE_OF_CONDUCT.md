# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in the Useful community a harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We will act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and
healthy community.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive community include:

- demonstrating empathy and kindness;
- respecting differing opinions, experiences, and technical backgrounds;
- giving and accepting constructive, specific feedback;
- taking responsibility, apologizing, and learning when mistakes affect others;
- focusing on what is best for the community and the people using the software.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- sexualized language, imagery, attention, or advances;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks;
- public or private harassment, threats, stalking, or intimidation;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- sustained disruption of discussion, review, or community events;
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith;
- other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Responsibilities and scope

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. They may edit, reject, or
remove contributions and restrict participation when behavior is inappropriate, threatening,
offensive, or harmful. Enforcement decisions should be consistent, proportionate, documented
privately, and respectful of reporter confidentiality.

This Code applies in repository spaces and in public spaces when an individual is officially
representing Useful, such as through an official account, event, or appointed representative role.

## Reporting and enforcement

A community may accept contributions only after a private conduct-reporting channel and its
enforcement team have been named, documented, and tested. Until then, public contribution intake
must remain closed. Do not submit conduct reports through the security vulnerability channel unless
the report also presents a security risk.

Once configured, reports should be handled promptly, impartially, and with only the disclosure
needed to investigate and protect affected people. A reporter will not be required to confront the
subject of a report directly.

Possible responses include a private correction, warning, temporary participation limit, removal
from community spaces, or a permanent ban, depending on impact and pattern. Good-faith reporting
must not result in retaliation.
